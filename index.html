The work you already do — minus everything that wastes your time

Most clipping work fails editors in the same four places: no steady supply, vague pay, counts you have to argue for, and rules you only learn after your account is hurt. This desk is built to remove all four.

No guesswork

What a brief actually contains

This is the shape of every brief — the same fields, every time, so you can start cutting in minutes instead of decoding a vague message.

CLIP BRIEFBrief # PILOT-___-E__ · One brief per clip, per editor

The source

Full title + link, who is speaking, and the rights-record reference. No rights record, no clipping — a brief with that field blank is not to be worked.

The cut

Timestamp range + the moment in one line + the exact hook line the clip must lead with. You may tighten inside the range; you do not extend outside it without asking.

Caption & disclosure

What the caption must include, what it must not include, hashtags, and the paid-work disclosure wording in the platform's required form.

Format & window

Platform, vertical 9:16, length band, caption style, reference clips that set the quality bar — and your posting window and deadline.

§ What makes this clip distinct — yours alone

The specific angle reserved to you: the hook treatment, framing, caption voice, or part of the range you emphasise. Every other editor on the same source holds a different angle. If your finished edit starts converging on another editor's clip of the same moment, you re-cut before posting.

Plain dealing

What counts — and what does not

The same rules that keep pay honest are the rules that keep your earnings defensible. Nothing here is discovered later, at your expense.

Counts toward your pay

  • Views read from your post link at the 7-day checkpoint — that is the payable count
  • Original edits, posted from your own declared account, inside the brief's window
  • Clips that pass the duplicate hash-check as Clear
  • Every verified clip, entered as its own line in the weekly ledger

Never counts — no exceptions

  • Identical reposting — a duplicate file is automatic non-payment, and one breach of the originality rule ends the engagement
  • Purchased views, purchased engagement, or patterns no organic post produces
  • Work from bought, farmed, or borrowed accounts — not eligible, ever
  • A moment clipped out of context, or a brief with no rights record behind it

Your part is deliberately small: cut it, post it, submit the link the same day. The desk does the counting, the duplicate check, and the ledger.

Straight answers

Questions editors ask

Do I need a big account to be accepted?

No follower minimum is stated in the pilot terms. You are judged on your sample clips, your account standing, and your reliability — and tiered on acceptance on that basis. A real account with real cuts is the requirement; bought or farmed accounts are excluded outright.

Can I clip content from outside the desk?

Not for this desk. You work only from briefs, and a brief is issued only when a written rights record covers the source — Game Phace Ent's own content, or a client's content under their grant. No rights record, no clipping.

What if another editor clips the same moment?

That is expected — and designed for. Each editor on the same source receives a different brief with a different reserved angle, so fifty clips of one moment are fifty different clips. If your edit converges on another editor's published clip, you re-cut before posting.

When and how do I get paid?

Weekly, from the ledger. Views are read at 48 hours and 7 days; the 7-day verified count is payable at your tier's settled rate — Entry $0.20, Established $0.25, Anchor $0.35 per 1,000 verified views. No payout moves without Game Phace Ent's approval of that week's ledger — that approval step is by design, and it is what makes the ledger the single, checkable record of what you are owed.

What is the pilot budget cap?

The pilot runs under a fixed budget cap of $500 for the 30-day pilot period, covering editor payouts across Game Phace Ent's own content and client trials combined. Briefs are issued within that cap, so work is not assigned beyond what the pilot funds. When the cap is reached, new briefs pause until the next period or a revised cap is set.

What are the settled rates?

Per 1,000 verified views: Entry (Tier 1) $0.20, Established (Tier 2) $0.25, Anchor (Tier 3) $0.35. You are paid at the tier you hold when the clip verifies, on the 7-day count.

Do I have to disclose that it is paid work?

Yes — always, in the platform's required form. The brief carries the exact disclosure wording for each clip. Paid work disclosed as paid work is a standing rule, not a per-clip choice.

What ends the engagement?

One thing ends it immediately, with no warning and no cure: a breach of the originality rule — an identical repost. Bought or farmed accounts are never eligible in the first place. Everything else is governed by the editor agreement you sign on acceptance.
